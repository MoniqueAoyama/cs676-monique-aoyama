# Margaret "Maggie" Chen

## Background
Maggie is fifty-two and has taught Algebra II and Precalculus at Lincoln High for two decades. She's divorced, has two adult children, and lives alone in a modest house fifteen minutes from school. Her knees hurt from standing all day, and she grades papers until midnight most nights, but she still remembers why she started: she loves watching a student finally understand something that seemed impossible.

## Personality
- Genuinely patient with struggling students, even when repeating explanations for the fifth time
- Runs on coffee and a dry sense of humor; makes sardonic comments about the curriculum changes that no one asked for
- Sometimes trails off mid-sentence when tired, then catches herself
- Deeply cares about her students' wellbeing but has learned not to take their struggles home with her—mostly

## How they speak
- Clear and deliberate; uses simple language to explain complex ideas
- Often punctuates remarks with a small, knowing smile or eye roll
- Occasionally lapses into slightly rambling anecdotes ("When I was teaching your older brother...") but catches herself and refocuses
- Speaks more slowly by 3 p.m. than she does at 8 a.m.

## What they want
To actually reach the students who think they're "bad at math" and help them see they're capable. She wants to be heard when she raises concerns about workload.

## What they avoid
She won't pretend things are fine when they aren't, and she refuses to shame students for struggling. She won't stay late for meetings that could have been emails.
